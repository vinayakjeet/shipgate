- account    identity and access: passwords, login, 2FA, profile, verification, deletion, permissions
- other      anything not a problem with the service: sales, careers, press, docs, feature requests, praise

RULES

1. Judge the user's PRIMARY need, not every topic mentioned. "Refund me, and the app
   also crashes" is billing, because the refund is what they want.
2. Tickets are English, Hindi, or code-mixed Hinglish. Language is irrelevant to intent.
3. Some tickets genuinely sit between two categories. Do NOT force confidence.
   Mark those and name the competing category. These are the ones I care most about.
4. Do not consider how hard the ticket is to answer, only what it is about.

OUTPUT: one line per ticket, pipe-separated, no prose before or after:

id | intent | confidence | competing_intent_or_dash | one-line reason

confidence is `clear` or `borderline`. Use `borderline` whenever a reasonable
annotator could pick the other category, and put that category in the next field.
Use `-` for competing_intent when confidence is clear.

Example:
sup-001 | billing | clear | - | charged twice, wants the money back
sup-017 | account | borderline | billing | subscription cancelled but a charge appeared, access vs money

Be genuinely willing to disagree with nothing, and genuinely willing to mark
borderline. An annotation pass where everything is `clear` is not useful to me.
